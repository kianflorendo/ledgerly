## Hypotheses
Small software agencies with milestone billing need better reconciliation of contracts, delivery and authorized acceptance.
Buyer: owner/finance lead. Existing tools may already suffice.
Agentic branching demonstrates technical behavior, not novelty or defensibility.
Potential future advantage: workflow quality, useful integrations, customer trust and outcome history. None exists yet.

## Pricing and economics
Test $99-249/business/month after a pilot; no validated price.
100 customers x $149 x 12 = $178,800 ARR; 1,000 = $1,788,000 ARR.
These are arithmetic scenarios, not TAM/SAM/SOM, forecasts or valuations.

At an assumed 12% annual financing cost, $15,000 paid 14 days earlier yields about $69 financing benefit. Earlier cash is not new revenue.
Time savings, cash acceleration and avoided loss must be measured separately.

## Solo discovery
Timebox five initial interviews through existing contacts. No outreach has been sent.
Ask for the most recent delayed invoice, stated vs actual blocker, resolution steps, evidence locations, active/elapsed time, existing tools, and consented redacted examples.
Only after demonstrating scope, ask for a concrete paid-pilot commitment.
Record observed examples separately from opinions and estimated time. Keep customer material outside the public repository.

Proposed gates:
- Three interviewees report recurring document-investigation blockers.
- Two can supply usable redacted examples.
- Two commit to a concrete paid pilot; interest alone is insufficient.

## Metrics and tripwire
Primary: time to correctly prepared, reviewer-approved action.
DSO and actual payment gains require real pilots; never derive them from synthetic cases.
Reconsider segment if delays are mainly inability to pay, tools already satisfy buyers, or usable evidence is inaccessible.
Review after first three interviews and feasibility gate.
